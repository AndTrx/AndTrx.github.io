A SMALL WINDOW INTO MY RESEARCH

Lighting up
the gas between galaxies.

The coloured structures are extended Lyman-alpha (Lyα) nebulae around quasars. They reveal some of the otherwise faint hydrogen surrounding galaxies: the circumgalactic medium (CGM). On larger scales, such observations can also probe gas connecting galaxies in the intergalactic medium (IGM). Quasar nebulae and the CGM ↗

This emission lets us investigate the reservoirs of gas involved in galaxy growth and the influence of an active galactic nucleus. A bright feature alone does not establish that gas is flowing in or out.

An image with a spectrum at every position

An integral field unit (IFU) feeds light from many positions on the sky into a spectrograph at once. MUSE, on ESO’s Very Large Telescope, produces a datacube with two spatial axes and a wavelength axis. You can inspect a local spectrum, then select wavelengths to build a different view of the gas. Explore IFUs with ESO ↗

What is a QSO?

A quasar, also called a quasi-stellar object (QSO), is an exceptionally luminous active galactic nucleus powered by gas accreting onto a supermassive black hole. Radiation from the hot accretion flow can illuminate gas far beyond the host galaxy. More about quasars ↗

Why does the gas emit Lyα?

Ionizing photons can remove electrons from hydrogen atoms. As electrons recombine and cascade through atomic energy levels, some emit Lyα photons: this is fluorescent recombination emission. Collisional excitation can also generate Lyα as electrons transfer energy to hydrogen. Their relative contributions depend on the gas and radiation field.

Lyα is ultraviolet light at its source. Cosmic expansion shifts the line to the optical wavelengths measured here. The heat palette is a visual aid, not the gas’s true colour. Emission mechanisms ↗

A resonant line, with a complicated journey

Lyα photons can scatter repeatedly through neutral hydrogen, changing direction and frequency before escaping. Some extended light can therefore be redistributed radiation originally emitted by the quasar or galaxies, rather than light produced at the apparent location.

That resonance makes the line rich in information, but its shape cannot be translated directly into gas velocity or density. Other emission lines and physical models help disentangle the processes. The maps here show masked emission; the local spectra retain noise and negative values. Read the review ↗

Data, display choices & scientific limitations

Wavelength: original linear FITS AWAV axis (air wavelengths), 1.25 Å per channel. All channels of each supplied cutout are available. The green band and persistent numerical limits show the full spectral extent of object ID 1, independently of the selected image window. No systemic redshift or velocity zero point is assumed.

Signal: original numerical data units. The input cube does not supply BUNIT, so no physical flux or surface-brightness units are asserted. Circular apertures include spaxels whose centres lie within the selected radius (0–8 pixels, or 0–1.6 arcsec); radius zero selects one spaxel. Apertures sum finite samples without spectral smoothing or normalization. No variance cube was supplied; the plot has no uncertainty estimates.

Spatial coordinates: 0.2 arcsec per pixel, north up and east left. The image, cube and mask share the same spatial grid and WCS. Readouts use one-based FITS pixel coordinates. The sky coordinate refers to the central spaxel and is calculated from the FITS TAN projection.

Display: the background and overlay use independent asinh stretches for visibility. The overlay uses a heat palette and mask-normalized Gaussian smoothing (σ = 1.2 pixels) for display. These affect only the images, not the plotted spectrum. Overlay transparency is zero outside the mask footprint. The colour overlay is not a calibrated surface-brightness map.

Web data: the original FITS files are not uploaded. This preview uses a spatial crop padded for the largest aperture and all channels in each supplied cutout, stored as float32 without binning or rounding beyond the original float32 precision.
